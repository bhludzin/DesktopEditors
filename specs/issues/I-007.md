# I-007: An a:ln with no w (and no p:style lnRef) is stroked at 12700 EMU (1 pt) instead of PowerPoint's 9525 EMU (0.75 pt)

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | open |
| Severity | minor |
| Slides | 136 |
| Findings | 153 |
| Categories | shape-fill-line |
| Confidence in the cause | high |

## What differs

The most common outline difference in the deck: 153 findings on 136 slides (6-225, 410-420, 585-648, 682-706); part 2 counted 436 such objects on 191 slides. Every affected object has <a:ln> with only a solidFill (no w attribute) and no p:style/lnRef: mostly the black outlines of the yellow FFFFCC/FFFF99 quote and label boxes, the white Tithes/Fast Offerings labels, red frames on 201-205 and 8 pictures. Euro-Office strokes them 1.33x too wide. Both PDFs go through the same aliased rasterizer (Windows.Data.Pdf, fidelity/windows/pdf-to-png.ps1), which lights a device pixel when its centre is inside the stroke, so a 2.22-px stroke (1600-px render) lights 2-3 rows or columns where PowerPoint's 1.67-px stroke lights 1-2; at 800 px an edge on a pixel boundary becomes 2 px instead of 1. Which edges differ depends only on where each edge falls on the pixel grid, which is why reviewers first called it 'snapping' and why each finding names only some edges. Pixel snapping is NOT a separate cause. Re-checked in the PDFs (pages 86, 114, 213, 417, 633, 702): PowerPoint writes '0.75 w' and Euro-Office '0.99998 w' for exactly these outlines. A 0.5 pixel-centre coverage rule from the xfrm reproduces every PowerPoint edge with 0.75 pt and every Euro-Office edge with 1 pt at 800 and 1600 px (S0114-2, S0148-2, S0120-2). Rotated, antialiased boxes integrate to 1.11-1.12 vs 0.83-0.84 sheet px (= 12700/9525, slides 632/633). Same-geometry outlines with an explicit w=9525 match pixel for pixel (slides 141-147, 417, 586, 634; Euro-Office's PDF writes '0.74999 w' there). Group scaling plays no part (704/706). The only 'snapping' leftovers are two exact pixel-centre ties decided by PowerPoint's PDF rounding coordinates to 0.01 pt (rejected S0332-1, S0402-1). Effect: some frame edges are 2-3x heavier, visible at 800 px (e.g. S0031-1, S0086-2, S0119-2).

## What PowerPoint does

An <a:ln> with no w and no style lnRef is drawn at 9525 EMU = 0.75 pt: its PDF writes '0.75 w' (with '1 j') for every such outline checked (pages 6, 86, 88, 114, 188, 191, 201-225, 410-420, 597-648, 682-706). That is 1.67 device px at 1600 px wide (5715 EMU/px) and 0.83 px at 800 px. With a p:style lnRef the theme lnStyleLst width applies (idx 1 = 9525; idx 2 = 25400 gives '2 w' on slide 701 even though spPr a:ln has no w), and Euro-Office already gets that case right.

## What Euro-Office does

Uses 12700 EMU (1 pt) when the width is missing: its PDF writes '0.99998 w', 2.22 device px at 1600 px and 1.11 px at 800 px. Edges whose centre falls near a pixel centre or boundary gain one or two fully black rows or columns, so frames look heavier when zoomed and slightly bolder at normal size.

## Suspected cause

CShape.prototype.recalculatePen leaves pen.w null when there is no p:style lnRef and spPr.ln has no w (pen = new CLn() merged with the compiled line). CShapeDrawer.fromShape (called by fromShape2) then substitutes 1 pt: 'this.StrokeWidth = (this.Ln.w == null) ? 12700 : parseInt(this.Ln.w)'. The same 12700 default is repeated in the other consumers of pen.w: the bounds checker, the shadow shape's pen padding, Word-shape insets, hit testing, ShapeToImageConverter and the asc stroke object shown in the UI.

## Where to look

sdkjs word/Drawing/ShapeDrawer.js: CShapeDrawer.fromShape (line 1439, reached through fromShape2 at 1276). Pen compiled in common/Drawings/Format/Shape.js: CShape.prototype.recalculatePen (line 3121), also used by pictures via common/Drawings/Format/Image.js: CImageShape.prototype.recalculatePen (line 362). Same 12700 fallback in common/Drawings/CommonController.js: CSlideBoundsChecker.prototype.CheckLineWidth (10450), common/Drawings/Format/GraphicObjectBase.js: recalculateShdw (1198), Shape.js 4657 (bWordShape insets) and 6460 (hit test), ShapeDrawer.js 2659 (ShapeToImageConverter), common/Drawings/Format/Format.js 16836 (asc stroke width for the UI) and slide/Editor/Format/Slide.js 1952 (commented out).

## Fix idea

Resolve the default once when the pen is compiled: at the end of CShape.prototype.recalculatePen, after this.pen.merge(oCompiledLine), add 'if (this.pen && this.pen.w == null) this.pen.w = 9525;' (limit it to presentation shapes and pictures, e.g. !this.bWordShape, unless DOCX is verified to use the same default). Every consumer (drawer, bounds, shadow, hit test, UI) then sees 0.75 pt. For pens that bypass recalculatePen, also change the '== null ? 12700' fallbacks listed above to 9525. Verify: pages 86, 114 and 213 of the Euro-Office PDF should show '0.75 w', and slides 112-114, 148, 206-225 and 597-648 should match pixel for pixel, as the explicit-w=9525 slides already do.

## How to verify

Build and render with `python fidelity/fidelity.py loop <deck> --label <name>`, then rebuild the review facts with `python fidelity/review_prep.py <deck-dir> <name>` and look at the slides below: their findings should be gone, and no other slide should lose line-break matches or SSIM against the previous run (`fidelity.py runs <deck>`). Then set `status` and `fixed_in` in `issues.json`, add a `log` entry, and re-run `review_record.py`.
