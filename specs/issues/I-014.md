# I-014: Outer shadow blurRad is ignored: the shadow is painted as a hard-edged flat silhouette

| | |

| Findings | 5 |
| Categories | shape-fill-line |

## What differs

5 findings on slides 186, 187, 194, 195 and 702; 11 slides in the deck use outerShdw with a non-zero blurRad. The shadow's offset and extent match; only the blur is missing. On slide 702 (picture on white; blurRad 4 pt, dist 3 pt at 45 degrees, 40% black) Euro-Office paints a flat band of luminance 153, exactly 2 sheet px wide, right of and below the frame. PowerPoint fades from 163 to 254 over about 6 px. The picture therefore looks as if it has a thick dark border on two sides (minor). On 186-195, the 50%-black text panel (Rectangle 4; blurRad 50800, dist 38100, dir 2700000, 40% black) casts its shadow onto a dark photo. There Euro-Office shows a flat band (luminance 35, 35, 35, 35, then a jump of 9-17 levels) where PowerPoint fades over about 4 px. That shows only in the difference map (trivial).

## What PowerPoint does

Draws an outer shadow whose blurRad is above 0 as an image with a soft mask. The image holds the shadow's silhouette: the object moved by dist at dir and scaled by sx/sy about algn, a picture's outline included (it lies outside the picture), in the shadow colour, its alpha the shadow's alpha times the fill's. The silhouette is blurred with a Gaussian whose standard deviation is blurRad / 3 (fitted: 0.332 to 0.337 x blurRad from 0.5 to 32 pt), and the image reaches blurRad past it. Its resolution is 200 dpi divided by ceil(3 x blurRad / 8 pt): 200 dpi up to 2 pt, 100 at 3 and 4 pt, 66.7 at 6 and 8, 40 at 12, 33.3 at 16, 22.2 at 24, 16.7 at 32. With rotWithShape="1" the direction turns with the shape. A shadow at distance 0 is not grown; it shows only through its blur. The silhouette comes out about a third of a raster pixel narrower on the right and bottom (0.15 pt at 200 dpi, 1.3 pt at 16.7 dpi). A filled text box also gets a shadow of its text, hidden under the fill.

## What Euro-Office does

Before the fix: filled the offset silhouette with the shadow colour at its alpha ('0 0 0 rg /E2 gs f' with ca 0.4), so its edge was hard. A shadow at distance 0 was grown by 1 mm so that it showed as a hard border, a picture's shadow left out the picture's outline, and rotWithShape was ignored.

## Suspected cause

Confirmed. recalculateShdw builds shdwSp, a plain CShape with the object's geometry and the shadow fill, and drawShdw drew it with the shadow transform. outerShdw.blurRad was read but never used when drawing.

## Where to look

sdkjs common/Drawings/Format/GraphicObjectBase.js: drawShdw calls drawSlideBlurredShadow (getSlideShadowRaster, drawSlideShadowSilhouette, blurSlideShadowAlpha, getSlideBlurredShadowImage); recalculateShdw (a picture's outline; no 1 mm growth on slides) and getShdwTransform (rotWithShape).

## Fix idea

Done in the presentation editor: a shadow with blurRad above 0 is rasterized at PowerPoint's resolution, its alpha blurred, and drawn as one image. In the converter (x2t) the silhouette is drawn with the native raster graphics (CNativeGraphics), read back with CZipEmbed.decodeImage, and the blurred image goes to the PDF as a PNG data URL, which the PDF writer embeds with a soft mask. On screen it is drawn on a canvas. The image is cached on the shadow shape. blurRad 0 keeps the vector fill.
