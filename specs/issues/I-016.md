## What differs

The two-line 96 pt Times New Roman title (anchor ctr, overflows its 128.6 px box, 100% spacing) is drawn 0.7-1.0 px lower in Euro-Office. PDF baselines: 187.54 / 302.76 pt in PowerPoint vs 188.27 / 303.47 pt in Euro-Office, so the pitch (115.2 pt = 1.2 x 96) is identical and the whole block is +0.73 pt (0.0076 em). For Calibri, first baselines match within 0.01 pt (e.g. 139.03 vs 139.04 on 718), so the split of the 1.2 em line into above/below-baseline parts is font-dependent. (Line 2 also starts 3.7 pt further left in EO, a horizontal spc=-300 / advance issue for the glyph group.)

## What PowerPoint does

First baseline of a 96 pt TNR line about 0.73 pt higher within the same centred block.

## What Euro-Office does

Places the baseline lower in the line box for Times New Roman (likely a different ascent source, e.g. hhea ascender + lineGap vs OS/2 win metrics).

## Suspected cause

Font-metric choice for the ascent part of a single-spaced line (TextAscent = Ascent + LineGap in EO) differs from PowerPoint's for fonts whose hhea/typo/win metrics disagree (Times New Roman), while it happens to agree for Calibri.

## Where to look

Not located precisely. Candidates: word/Editor/Styles.js CTextPr.GetTextMetrics (15269) / CTextMetrics.UpdateByFontInfo (font metric source), and word/Editor/Run.js Recalculate_LineMetrics where textAscent = Ascent + LineGap (about 5189-5216).

## Fix idea

Compare EO's Ascent/Descent/LineGap for Times New Roman with its hhea, OS/2 typo and win tables, and derive PowerPoint's rule from Calibri (first baseline 0.936 em = winAscent x 1.2/(winAscent+winDescent)) plus a TNR test. Then compute the slide-text baseline offset in the line from the same table PowerPoint uses.
