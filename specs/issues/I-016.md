## What differs

The two-line 96 pt Times New Roman title (anchor ctr, overflows its 128.6 px box, 100% spacing) is drawn 0.7-1.0 px lower in Euro-Office. PDF baselines: 187.54 / 302.76 pt in PowerPoint vs 188.27 / 303.47 pt in Euro-Office, so the pitch (115.2 pt = 1.2 x 96) is identical and the whole block is +0.73 pt (0.0076 em). For Calibri, first baselines match within 0.01 pt (e.g. 139.03 vs 139.04 on 718), so the split of the 1.2 em line into above/below-baseline parts is font-dependent. (Line 2 also starts 3.7 pt further left in EO, a horizontal spc=-300 / advance issue for the glyph group.)

## What PowerPoint does

A font's 1.2 em line is split above and below the baseline at its OS/2 win ascent : win descent (at its typo ascent : descent with the typo line gap above, when USE_TYPO_METRICS is set). The hhea line gap does not count. Times New Roman (win 1825/443) puts 0.8047 of the line above the baseline: 92.70 pt at 96 pt (PDF 92.82, on the 0.12 pt grid) and 17.38 at 18 pt (17.41); Arial 17.50 (17.53). Measured on 34 fonts at 18 pt and Times New Roman at 48 and 96 pt (test decks mixfont-rules and mixfont-rules2).

## What Euro-Office does

Before the fix: split the line at the font's line height, the larger of the hhea height (with its line gap) and the win ascent plus descent, and put the difference above the baseline. Times New Roman (hhea line gap 87 units) sat 0.13 pt low at 18 pt and 0.71 at 96 pt; Arial (gap 67) 0.09 at 18 pt. Calibri's hhea height equals its win height, so it matched.

## Suspected cause

Confirmed: the hhea line gap was counted in the split of the 1.2 em line.

## Where to look

slide/Drawing/DrawingDocument.js: GetSlideFontLineHeight, used by the slide CTextMeasurer.GetAscender and GetDescender.

## Fix idea

Compare EO's Ascent/Descent/LineGap for Times New Roman with its hhea, OS/2 typo and win tables, and derive PowerPoint's rule from Calibri (first baseline 0.936 em = winAscent x 1.2/(winAscent+winDescent)) plus a TNR test. Then compute the slide-text baseline offset in the line from the same table PowerPoint uses.
