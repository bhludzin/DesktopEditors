## What differs

Two boxes with an effective 80% line spacing are shifted up as a whole while the line pitch matches. One is the body placeholder on 579 (normAutofit fontScale=47500 lnSpcReduction=20000, 100% lnSpc; 16 lines, top-anchored). The other is the title text box on 594-596 (fontScale=85000 lnSpcReduction=10000 on lstStyle lnSpc 90%; one line, anchor ctr). PDF: slide 579 first baseline 142.39 pt in PowerPoint vs 142.00 in Euro-Office (-0.39 pt), every later line the same; slide 594 title 108.14 vs 107.79 (-0.35 pt). Scaled font sizes agree (15 pt and 28 pt in both PDFs), and at 100% spacing first baselines agree to 0.01 pt (e.g. slide 718).

## What PowerPoint does

Under percentage spacing other than 100%, and under exact spacing, a line is H high: the percentage of the text height (1.2 x the font size; the tallest text on a mixed line) or the exact value in whole points. A quarter of H lies below the baseline, plus however much the font's single-spacing descent exceeds a quarter of the single line height (Courier New); at single height or less the part below is never more than the font's descent. The rest lies above. 18 pt Calibri (single line 21.6 pt, descent 4.75): at 150% the first baseline is 24.3 pt below the box top (0.75 x 32.4), at 200% 32.4, at 80% 12.96 (a quarter, 4.32, is less than the descent), at 90% 14.69 (a quarter, 4.86, is more, so all 2.16 pt come off above). Exactly 100% keeps the font's own ascent (16.85), so 101% puts the first baseline about 0.5 pt higher than 100% does. Exact 22 pt spacing: 16.5. normAutofit lnSpcReduction is subtracted from the percentage, also from lnSpc set on the paragraph itself (100% - 20% = 80%, 150% - 20% = 130%); exact spacing is not reduced. For ctr and b anchoring (and the text height) the last line ends at its font descent below the baseline; the rest of its line height is not counted. PowerPoint then snaps baselines to a 1/600-inch grid, so single cases differ by up to 0.12 pt.

## What Euro-Office does

Before the fix: Word's rule. Above 100% the text kept its single-spacing ascent and the extra went below each line, so the first baseline stayed at the 100% position (18 pt Calibri: 7.6 pt too high at 150%, 15.6 at 200%). Below 100% the whole reduction came off above the line (0.44 pt too high at 80%, 2.1 at 50%). Exact spacing put the whole difference above the baseline (5.2 pt too low at 40 pt). The last line's extra space counted in the text height, so ctr- and b-anchored 18 pt text at 150% sat 9.3 and 11.0 pt too high. lnSpcReduction applied only to inherited spacing, not to lnSpc set on the paragraph.

## Suspected cause

Confirmed: Euro-Office placed lines under line spacing by Word's rule (the text keeps its ascent and the whole difference goes below the line above 100%, above it below 100%, and above the baseline under exact spacing), counted the last line's extra space in the text height, and ignored lnSpcReduction when lnSpc was set on the paragraph itself.

## Where to look

word/Editor/Paragraph_Recalculate.js: CParaLineMetrics.ApplySlideLineSpacing, IsSlideLineSpacing and GetAutoLineSpacing, called from Paragraph.private_RecalculateLineMetrics; private_RecalculateLinePosition (the last line's bottom); CParaLineMetrics.Recalculate_LineGap (lnSpcReduction, upstream 0c5d320b17).

## Fix idea

Build a small test deck (Calibri 15/28 pt; lnSpc 100/90/80% with and without lnSpcReduction) and compare PowerPoint's first baseline with EO's. Then change the first-line reduction for slide text to match: e.g. scale only the part of the line above the ascent, and decide between subtractive (lnSpc - reduction) and multiplicative (lnSpc x (1 - reduction)) combination.

## How to verify

Build and render with `python fidelity/fidelity.py loop <deck> --label <name>`, then rebuild the review facts with `python fidelity/review_prep.py <deck-dir> <name>` and look at the slides below: their findings should be gone, and no other slide should lose line-break matches or SSIM against the previous run (`fidelity.py runs <deck>`). Then set `status` and `fixed_in` in `issues.json`, add a `log` entry, and re-run `review_record.py`.
