# I-018: Centred 40 pt title starts about 0.36 pt left of PowerPoint's position

## What differs

The centred title 'The Beast Will Support The Gathering' is shifted left by about 0.4-0.5 px. It is a title placeholder with normAutofit fontScale=90000, 44 -> 40 pt in both PDFs, and kern=1200 from the master titleStyle. PDF line start: x 55.37 pt in PowerPoint vs 55.00 pt in Euro-Office. Centring on 360 pt implies EO's advance width for the line is about 0.73 pt larger, yet the ink extents measured in the renders have the same width (drift -0.39 left end, -0.17 middle, -0.50 right end). So the extra width is in non-ink advance: the last glyph's advance/side bearing, or a kerning or spacing value counted at the line end. The vertical position matches (80.09 vs 80.07).

## What PowerPoint does

Centres the line so that it starts at x 55.37 pt.

## What Euro-Office does

Starts at x 55.00 pt: the centred width it uses is about 0.73 pt larger than PowerPoint's.

## Suspected cause

Unclear. Candidates: a kerning pair or an end-of-line advance counted in Range.W (kern=1200 at 40 pt), or K2-type advance differences at 40 pt that cancel inside the line but not in the centring width.

## Where to look

Not located. Centring is in word/Editor/Paragraph_Recalculate.js private_RecalculateLineAlign (2043), and the line width comes from ParaRun.Recalculate_Range_Width (word/Editor/Run.js 5233).

## Fix idea

Dump Range.W and the per-glyph advances of this title in EO and compare with the PDF glyph positions of both renderers (TJ arrays) to find where the 0.73 pt comes from before changing anything.

## Open questions

- The issue lists K2-type advance differences as a candidate, but K2 can be excluded: the ink extents have the same width (within 0.11 px) while the centring width differs by about 0.73 pt, so the extra width lies outside the ink (line-start side bearing or trailing advance or kern at the line end). The title has no trailing space and no spc. Per-glyph advance drift would change the ink width.
