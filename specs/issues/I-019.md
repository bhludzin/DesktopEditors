## What differs

Slide 21 Arc 3 (prst arc, adj1 16671306 / adj2 0, rot 157.6 deg, 2.25 pt): the lower part of the stroke is displaced outward in Euro-Office. Measured stroke-centre x at 1600 px, in sheet px: no shift at y 390-400, +0.10 at 420, +0.14 at 460, +0.24 at 470, +0.19 at 480, and 0 again at the end (y 490). The reviewer's 0.5 px is overstated. Rebuilding both PDF curves gives a maximum normal deviation of 0.09 sheet px mid-segment, with the same sign and the same shape as the measurement. Both ends are identical. The difference grows with arc radius, up to about 0.05% of the radius per 90-degree segment.

## What PowerPoint does

PowerPoint's PDF emits the 76.1-degree (parametric) arc as one cubic: '138.7 96.802 m 95.883 97.92 46.428 145.67 18.846 212.52 c'. Its control points match exactly the standard tangent length k = 4/3 * tan(dTheta/4) = 0.45980 (dTheta = parametric sweep), applied as P1 = P0 + k*(-rx sin t1, ry cos t1).

## What Euro-Office does

Euro-Office emits one cubic with the same end points but control points (96.025, 97.916) and (46.337, 145.888). These match exactly Maisonobe's alpha = sin(dTheta) * (sqrt(4 + 3 tan^2(dTheta/2)) - 1) / 3 = 0.45827.

## Suspected cause

ArcTo.js EllipseArc3 computes the control-point length with Maisonobe's formula instead of the 4/3*tan(dTheta/4) kappa that PowerPoint uses. The full-ellipse helper also hard-codes c_fKappa = 0.552 instead of 0.5522847.

## Where to look

sdkjs common/Drawings/ArcTo.js: EllipseArc3 (line 210, fAlpha) and Ellipse (line 99, c_fKappa = 0.552); reached via Arc3/ArcToCurvers from common/Drawings/Format/Path.js and Geometry.js.

## Fix idea

Set fAlpha = 4 / 3 * Math.tan((dAngle2 - dAngle1) / 4) in EllipseArc3, and c_fKappa = 0.5522847498 in Ellipse (and in EllipseOnCanvas if it uses the same constant). Re-render slide 21 and check that the PDF control points equal PowerPoint's.
