## What differs

Found by the 3963-slide deck's review (run i024-c, sdkjs f53e0a4a15). Blip effects inside a:blip are parsed but not applied when drawing. 1484: clrChange makes white (and red ED1C24) transparent; Euro-Office keeps them, so the map's white background covers the top of the caption. 1046/1047: clrChange black-to-transparent plus a red duotone; Euro-Office shows black squares around the moons and the moon is not red. 2902 and 2906: a:lum contrast 20000 / 10000; PowerPoint's pictures have visibly higher contrast (measured 1.10-1.26x stretch on 2906). Slides (5): 1046-1047, 1484, 2902, 2906.

## What PowerPoint does

Applies the colour change (transparent key colour), duotone recolouring and luminance/contrast to the picture before drawing it.

## What Euro-Office does

Draws the original picture unchanged (only alphaModFix transparency is honoured).

## Suspected cause

CBlipFill.Effects holds CClrChange / CDuotone / CLumEffect objects, but the drawing path only consumes CAlphaModFix (CBlipFill.getTransparent); no pixel transform is done for the others.

## Where to look

sdkjs/common/Drawings/Format/Format.js (CBlipFill, CClrChange ~4623, CDuotone ~4697, CLumEffect ~5006); picture drawing in the shape drawer / native graphics

## Fix idea

Apply the blip effects to the decoded image before it is drawn or written to the PDF: clrChange as a colour-key alpha mask (with PowerPoint's tolerance), duotone as a luminance map between the two colours, lum as bright/contrast on each channel.

## Open questions

- The exact colour-key tolerance PowerPoint uses for clrChange was not measured.
