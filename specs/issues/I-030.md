## What differs

Found by the 3963-slide deck's review (run i024-c, sdkjs f53e0a4a15). Blip effects inside a:blip are parsed but not applied when drawing. 1484: clrChange makes white (and red ED1C24) transparent; Euro-Office keeps them, so the map's white background covers the top of the caption. 1046/1047: clrChange black-to-transparent plus a red duotone; Euro-Office shows black squares around the moons and the moon is not red. 2902 and 2906: a:lum contrast 20000 / 10000; PowerPoint's pictures have visibly higher contrast (measured 1.10-1.26x stretch on 2906). Slides (5): 1046-1047, 1484, 2902, 2906.

## What PowerPoint does

Bakes the effects into the picture's pixels. Every clrChange comes first and is matched against the original pixels, wherever it stands in the blip: a pixel within 1 of clrFrom in each channel takes clrTo's colour and alpha (clrTo with alpha 0 makes it transparent; 50% gives alpha 128). Then lum and duotone apply in the order the blip lists them. lum maps each channel v (0 to 1) to floor(255 x clip(k x (v - 1/2) + 1/2 + bright x (1 + k) / 2)), with k = 1 / (1 - contrast) for a positive contrast and 1 + contrast otherwise, so brightness alone adds and contrast alone pivots about mid-grey. duotone takes g = floor(0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B) of the sRGB values and gives round(c1 + (c2 - c1) x g / 255) per channel; its colours are worked out with their modifiers as for any colour (tint in linear light, satMod in HSL without capping the saturation: FF0000 tint 45% satMod 400% is (255, 107, 107)). The moon on 1046: black made transparent, then a black-to-light-red duotone, then lum bright 14% contrast 52%, so grey 128 becomes (183, 29, 29). The PDF holds the result as the picture's image, with a soft mask where it is transparent, encoded by K6's rule. Measured on the test deck blipfx-rules (lum sweeps, duotones, colour keys and orders on lossless ramps, exact to the level).

## What Euro-Office does

Before the fix: drew the original picture (only alphaModFix was honoured). Now, in slides, a picture or picture fill whose blip has clrChange, lum or duotone is drawn from a copy with PowerPoint's rules applied: the PDF path decodes the media file in x2t, applies the effects and passes the result to the writer as a PNG; the canvas path does the same from the loaded image. Results are cached per picture and effects. On the test deck every lossless image equals PowerPoint's to the level, and the JPEG ones differ by compression noise only.

## Suspected cause

Cause found: CBlipFill.Effects holds CClrChange, CDuotone and CLumEffect, but drawing only used CAlphaModFix (CBlipFill.getTransparent); nothing transformed the pixels, and picture brushes never worked out the effects' colours.

## Where to look

sdkjs common/Drawings/Format/GraphicObjectBase.js: getSlideBlipEffectsImage (getSlideBlipColorEffects resolves the colours from the shape's theme, slide, layout and master; applySlideBlipColorEffects; getSlideBlipSourcePixels reads the media file with native GetImagesPath / GetFileBinary and CZipEmbed decodeImage, or the loaded image on canvas). word/Drawing/ShapeDrawer.js: getBlipTextureSrc (PDF brush texture, stretched and tiled) and getBlipCanvas (canvas). Presentation editor only; SVG, EMF and WMF pictures are left alone.

## Fix idea

Done (see euro_office). Not done: clrChange's useA (alpha is not compared), the other blip effects (grayscl, biLevel, clrRepl, hsl, tint, alphaMod and the rest), and PowerPoint's downsampling of large pictures (I-025).

## Open questions

- The canvas path (the editor's screen view and thumbnails) uses the same pixel code but is not covered by the harness, which scores the PDF; a picture the browser will not let a canvas read keeps its original pixels.
- A semi-transparent pixel that matches clrChange gets its alpha multiplied by clrTo's; only opaque pixels were measured.
