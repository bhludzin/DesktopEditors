## What differs

Found by the 3963-slide deck's review (run i024-c, sdkjs f53e0a4a15). An incomplete fix: K6 (core 88cf859ae7) chooses the encoding. Pictures that PowerPoint writes losslessly (FlateDecode) are written by Euro-Office as JPEG (DCTDecode) at quality 85. Line art and screenshot text in them are visibly softer, with ringing (1078, 2660, 1077). Slides (32): 435, 796-797, 1077-1079, 1136, 1159, 1161-1164, 1484, 1551, 1555, 1954, 2163, 2166, 2657-2660, 2795-2796, 2903, 2969-2971, 3000, 3636, 3818-3819.

## What PowerPoint does

Lossless Flate image.

## What Euro-Office does

Follows K6's rule (core 88cf859ae7, LoadSlideImage): Flate when the picture has at most 256 colours or deflate is not larger than JPEG, otherwise JPEG at quality 75. On these slides JPEG is smaller (1078: 120412 bytes against PowerPoint's Flate 153894), so they are JPEG, and line art and text in the pictures are softer, with ringing (1078, 2660, 1077).

## Suspected cause

K6's encoding rule is incomplete. K6 already found two such exceptions in the 838-slide deck (image54 29 x 27 and image34 116 x 173, lossless though JPEG is smaller). Here all 32 are PNG sources, so PowerPoint may decide by the source format or by a size test other than deflate against JPEG at quality 75.

## Where to look

core PdfFile/PdfWriter.cpp: LoadSlideImage (K6, core 88cf859ae7 on pptx-pdf-fidelity).

## Fix idea

Collect every picture of the three decks with its source format, colour count and both encodings' sizes, and find the rule that separates PowerPoint's Flate from its JPEG choices; then change LoadSlideImage.

## Open questions

- The upstream core checkout in DesktopEditors/core writes JPEG at quality 85, but the run used K6's build (quality 75).
