## What differs

This is the K2 mechanism, here at more than 2 px. Line breaks match, but glyphs drift steadily along long lines. Regular Calibri at 13.5-18 pt ends 2.0-3.2 px early on 540-760 px lines (about 0.3-0.4% short, about 0.03 px per character). Bold 13.5 pt ends 1.5-2.5 px late (slides 541, 550/551, 631), while bold 12 pt ends about 2 px early (24, 192). URLs with many narrow glyphs drift faster (0.52%). 30 findings on 28 slides. A numeric check (work-glyphs-fonts/roundtest3.py, roundtest4.py, model-check.txt; calibri.ttf/calibrib.ttf hmtx+kern) shows that Euro-Office's line ends equal the exact sum of design advances plus kern pairs. For example, slide 97 'according to ... into' predicts an ink end of 774.1 px, and EO measures 774.0. PowerPoint's lines match a model where each glyph's advance is rounded to whole 1/576-inch units (ppem = pt x 8) and the kern adjustment is truncated toward zero. On 18 test lines that model predicts PPT-EO end offsets of +2.6/+2.5/+2.7/+2.1/+1.2/+3.7/+2.6 px (regular 18 pt; measured +3.0/3.0/3.0/3.0/1.5/3.0/2.5), +1.1/+0.8/+1.5/+1.7 (regular 13.5 pt; measured 2.0/2.0/1.5/3.0), -2.0 (bold 13.5 pt; measured -1.5), +2.8/+2.9 (bold 12 pt; measured +2.0/+2.0) and +1.8/+2.6/+1.9 for the three URLs (measured 2.0/2.5/2.0). RMS error is 0.70 px, against 2.5 px for linear advances. The model also gets the sign flip between bold 12 pt and bold 13.5 pt right, which no constant scale factor can. Not fully explained: small extra steps at run boundaries (slides 48 and 110, where PowerPoint's text after a colour-run change is up to 1 px wider than the same text in one run), and about 0.4 px at a baseline=30000 superscript (slide 509).

## What PowerPoint does

Lays out glyphs with advances quantized to its 576-dpi master-unit grid. Each glyph advance at ppem = 8 x pt is rounded to an integer, and pair kerning is applied as a truncated integer number of units. As a result line lengths deviate from linear design widths by a weight- and size-dependent amount: +0.35-0.4% for regular Calibri at 13.5/18 pt, -0.3/-0.5% for bold at 13.5 pt, +0.4% for bold at 12 pt.

## What Euro-Office does

Shapes text with HarfBuzz at 576 pt (FONTSIZE in engine.js) and scales the 26.6 advances linearly to the run size. Advances are therefore exact, unquantized design widths plus kerning, and the per-glyph rounding bias PowerPoint accumulates is missing.

## Suspected cause

Euro-Office measures slide text with the font's exact linear advances: HarfBuzz at MEASURE_FONTSIZE 576, scaled to the run size, with spaces from GetGraphemeWidth (engine.js HB_ShapeString, TextShaper.FlushGrapheme/private_HandleSpace). PowerPoint lays text out with per-glyph advances that differ from the linear hmtx widths by small amounts that depend on the glyph, size and weight. Where breaks match, this shows as drift: regular Calibri 13.5/18 pt about +0.35% wider in PowerPoint, bold 13.5 pt the other way (K2 tags; 30 findings over 2 px). On lines that end within that margin of the text edge, one word moves (91 findings in wrap-k3/1). EO's line is 0.26-2.6 pt narrower on the regular lines where it fits an extra word, and 0.7-2.1 pt wider on the bold lines where it fits one fewer. The best model so far rounds each advance to whole 1/576-inch units at the run size and truncates kern values. It gives RMS 0.70 px on 18 test lines, against 2.5 px for linear advances, and it matches the 13.5 pt regular space (grid 3.000 vs 2.997 pt measured). It does not match all of wrap-k3/1's per-glyph PDF data: the 13.5 pt bold space measures 2.78 pt against 3.00 predicted. The fixed 0.25 pt PRESENTATION_LINE_WIDTH_REDUCTION (Shape.js 4085) only compensates the regular-weight drift on average, and it must be re-fitted once the advances change (see K3).

## Where to look

sdkjs/common/libfont/engine.js: AscFonts.HB_ShapeString (554), where `nWidth += x_advance * COEF` (598) yields the width per 1 pt of font size; sdkjs/word/Editor/Paragraph/TextShaper.js: CParagraphTextShaper.FlushGrapheme (178), where `_nWidth` (202) is passed to private_HandleItem -> CRunText.SetWidth (word/Editor/Paragraph/RunContent/Text.js:295), which multiplies by the font size

## Fix idea

For slide (presentation) text only, quantize each grapheme's advance at the run's real size. Let u = 25.4/576 mm. Then width_mm = round(nominalAdvance_mm / u) * u + trunc(kernDelta_mm / u) * u. Do this in CParagraphTextShaper.FlushGrapheme, or pass the size into HB_ShapeString, and divide back by FontSize before SetWidth, because SetWidth stores per-pt widths times FontSize. The nominal advance is the glyph's hmtx advance (from an unkerned measurement via AscFonts.GetGraphemeWidth of a single-glyph grapheme). The kern delta is x_advance minus that nominal value. A simpler first try is round((advance+kern)/u)*u (RMS 1.15 px on the same lines, versus 0.70 for the full model and 2.5 today). Validate on the whole deck: the K2 drift tags and the K3 wraps should both shrink.

## Open questions

- The 1/576-inch per-advance rounding model fits the K2 line ends (RMS 0.70 px) and the regular 13.5 pt space, but not all of wrap-k3/1's per-glyph PowerPoint PDF data. At 13.5 pt the bold space measures 2.78 pt (model 3.00), the bold letters measure +0.03..+0.04 pt (model about -0.01), and regular 'o' measures 7.187 (model 7.125). Treat the rule as a hypothesis: validate it against both data sets before coding it, especially bold. Note also that PowerPoint's PDF sets 13.5 pt text at Tf 13.56 or 13.584, varying per line (page 125), so any analysis that uses PDF font sizes instead of glyph positions will be biased.
