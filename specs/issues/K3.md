# K3: Words that end within a few tenths of a point of the text edge: Euro-Office's fixed 0.25 pt line-width reduction rejects words PowerPoint keeps

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | open |
| Severity | major |
| Slides | 14 |
| Findings | 14 |
| Categories | text-wrap |
| Confidence in the cause | medium |

## What differs

13 findings where Euro-Office breaks one word EARLIER although its glyph positions on the line agree with PowerPoint's to within about 0.15 pt (or are narrower): bullets on the same box at three widths (S0011/S0013/S0014: 'Ephraim.', 'God'), quote lines (S0316 'ye', S0353 'the', S0386 'hastens', S0729 'to', S0775 'it', S0590/S0591/S0592 'somewhat'), the bold 18 pt oval labels (S0390 'Israel.') and the 22.6 pt-wide 'Lud' label (S0440, which Euro-Office then splits inside the word, Lu/d, because a single word that does not fit is broken by character). Every following line of the paragraph reflows; on S0390 the extra line overflows the 35 px oval and on S0440 'd' hangs below the white box.

## What PowerPoint does

PowerPoint keeps the word when its own rendered line ends between 0.35 pt inside the text edge (box minus lIns/rIns) and slightly past it: measured PowerPoint margins +0.35 (S0353), +0.30 (S0316, S0386), +0.20 (S0590-S0592), +0.15/-0.04 (S0011), +0.07 (S0013/S0014), +0.06 (S0729), -0.15 (S0390), -0.94 pt (S0775). Its tolerance is not a fixed width: in other boxes it wraps words whose rendered line would end 0.26-0.37 pt inside the edge (S0093, S0486, S0502, S0297, S0721), so its break measure is not exactly its rendered glyph positions.

## What Euro-Office does

Euro-Office lays slide text out on (box width - insets - 0.25 pt - 0.01 mm). On these lines its own line with the extra word would end 0.06-0.31 pt inside the text edge (S0440: 0.12 pt; S0390: about 0.1 pt past), i.e. inside the box but inside the 0.278 pt reduction zone, so the word is moved to the next line.

## Suspected cause

The constant PRESENTATION_LINE_WIDTH_REDUCTION (0.25 pt, plus TEXT_RECT_ERROR 0.01 mm) added in commit 7898a3e is a statistical compensation for the advance drift of wrap-k3/1; on lines without drift it makes Euro-Office stricter than PowerPoint. Without it, 12 of these 13 lines would match (all but S0390). PowerPoint's own acceptance band (-0.94..+0.37 pt) overlaps with its rejection band, so these are genuinely borderline and no single constant reproduces all of them.

## Where to look

common/Drawings/Format/Shape.js: CShape.recalculateDocContent (4081-4085: oContentW -= TEXT_RECT_ERROR + PRESENTATION_LINE_WIDTH_REDUCTION; constant at 970); fit test in word/Editor/Run.js ParaRun.Recalculate_Range (3868, 3949); mid-word split in Paragraph.FindLineBreakInLongWord (word/Editor/Paragraph_Recalculate.js:2723) called from Run.js:3899.

## Fix idea

Fix wrap-k3/1 first, then re-fit the reduction against PowerPoint's PDF line ends: with PowerPoint-compatible advances the measured accept/reject margins suggest a much smaller value (about 0-0.1 pt) instead of 0.25 pt. Removing the reduction on its own fixes these 12 lines but, per the commit message, costs about 1.5% of matched lines elsewhere because the advance drift is then uncompensated, so do it together with wrap-k3/1. For single words wider than the line in tiny label boxes (S0440), consider letting a word that overflows by less than the reduction stay whole rather than splitting it by character.

## Also covers

Issues found separately that share this cause:

- A single word that fits a narrow box is split mid-word, because the fixed 0.25 pt line-width reduction makes it too wide (code: common/Drawings/Format/Shape.js: CShape.prototype.recalculateDocContent (3929), oContentW -= TEXT_RECT_ERROR + PRESENTATION_LINE_WIDTH_REDUCTION at 4085 (constant at 970); word/Editor/Run.js: ParaRun.prototype.Recalculate_Range long-word branch lines 3867-3931 calling word/Editor/Paragraph_Recalculate.js Paragraph.prototype.FindLineBreakInLongWord (2723).)

## Open questions

- Cause looks right but is coupled to K2: the 0.25 pt reduction is the compensation for K2's drift, so removing it alone regresses about 1.5% of lines (per commit 7898a3e), and fixing K2 alone does not change these 13 lines. K3 and K2 should land in the same change set and be re-measured together.

## How to verify

Build and render with `python fidelity/fidelity.py loop <deck> --label <name>`, then rebuild the review facts with `python fidelity/review_prep.py <deck-dir> <name>` and look at the slides below: their findings should be gone, and no other slide should lose line-break matches or SSIM against the previous run (`fidelity.py runs <deck>`). Then set `status` and `fixed_in` in `issues.json`, add a `log` entry, and re-run `review_record.py`.
