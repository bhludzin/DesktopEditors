## What differs

Underlines sit about 0.6 px higher and are about 0.1 px thinner than PowerPoint's (1600 px renders). Recorded as a slide tag.

## What PowerPoint does

Draws underlines and strikethroughs as filled bars from the face's own metrics, on a 1/600-inch (0.12 pt) grid counted from the text box: the baseline is rounded down to the grid; the underline top is round(-post.underlinePosition / unitsPerEm x size) steps below it and the bar is round(post.underlineThickness / unitsPerEm x size) steps thick; the strikethrough top is round(OS/2 yStrikeoutPosition / unitsPerEm x size) steps above it and round(yStrikeoutSize / unitsPerEm x size) steps thick. Calibri: underline -232/134, strike 512/134, bold strike 512/186, so bold strikes are thicker; Times New Roman Bold and Arial Bold have thicker underlines. A strikethrough follows each run (a superscript's at its own 2/3 size and raised baseline), and so does a subscript's underline. A superscript's underline stays on the line's baseline at the full run size. Touching underlines on one baseline are drawn as one bar whose top and bottom are the width-weighted means of the runs' own, each rounded (13.5 + 24 pt Calibri: 13-20 and 23-36 steps below the baseline become 17-26). An underline alone on a line with larger plain text uses its own run's metrics. dblStrike draws two bars of the strike's thickness, directly above and below the single strike's place.

## What Euro-Office does

Before the fix: the strike centred 0.27 em above the baseline and the underline 0.4 x the line's text descent below it, both fontSize/18 pt thick for every face and weight (Calibri 13.5 pt: 0.75 pt instead of 0.84, about 0.5 pt too high). The PDF renderer centred the stroke on that y while the screen drew it from the top.

## Suspected cause

ParagraphLineDrawState.updateStrikeoutUnderlinePos (word/Editor/Paragraph/draw/line-draw-state.js 544-569) places the strike at 0.27 em above the baseline. It places the underline at UnderlineOffset = 0.4 x line TextDescent (word/Editor/Paragraph.js 3169), and gives both the same thickness lineW = fontSize/18 pt. PowerPoint uses the face's own metrics: the underline top at post.underlinePosition with thickness about post.underlineThickness (Calibri -232/134 units), and the strike from OS/2 yStrikeoutPosition/yStrikeoutSize (Calibri 512/134, Calibri Bold 512/186). PDF page 125 (13.5 pt Calibri, baseline 342.86): PowerPoint fills the underline band 340.50-341.34, i.e. top 1.52 pt below the baseline (post predicts 1.53) and 0.84 pt thick (post 0.88). Euro-Office strokes 0.75 pt centred at 341.44, which is 0.52 pt (0.58 sheet px) higher and 0.09 pt (0.1 px) thinner: exactly the K4 tag. The strike in S0096-2 is 1 px too high and not thicker for bold, for the same reason.

## Where to look

word/Editor/Paragraph/draw/line-draw-state.js: ParagraphLineDrawState.updateStrikeoutUnderlinePos, updateSlideStrikeoutUnderlinePos; word/Editor/Paragraph.js: drawRunContentLines, CParaDrawingRangeHorizontalLines.BlendSlideUnderlines; word/Editor/Styles.js: CTextPr.GetDecorationMetrics; common/libfont/file.js: GetFontDecorationMetrics; common/libfont/manager.js: CFontFilesCache.LoadFontFile.

## Also covers

Issues found separately that share this cause:

- Strikethrough uses a fixed 0.27 em position and fontSize/18 thickness instead of the font's OS/2 strikeout metrics (about 1 px too high, not thicker for bold) (code: sdkjs/word/Editor/Paragraph/draw/line-draw-state.js: ParagraphLineDrawState.updateStrikeoutUnderlinePos (544): `let strikeoutShift = 0.27;` (550) and `let lineW = (fontSize / 18) * g_dKoef_pt_to_mm;` (562))

## How to verify

Build and render with `python fidelity/fidelity.py loop <deck> --label <name>`, then rebuild the review facts with `python fidelity/review_prep.py <deck-dir> <name>` and look at the slides below: their findings should be gone, and no other slide should lose line-break matches or SSIM against the previous run (`fidelity.py runs <deck>`). Then set `status` and `fixed_in` in `issues.json`, add a `log` entry, and re-run `review_record.py`.
