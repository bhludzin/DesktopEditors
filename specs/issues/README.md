## Rejected findings

A second look judged these not to be real Euro-Office differences.

- S0021-2 (slide [21](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0021.png)): Not a real Euro-Office difference. PDF baselines of the affected lines match within 0.02-0.03 pt: PowerPoint's own pitch alternates 16.20/16.23 pt while EO's is a steady 16.20, e.g. 'anger.' at 261.17 vs 261.15 pt. The 0.25 px measured in the 800-px sheet comes from rasterizer snapping (K5 level at most).
- S0076-3 (slide [76](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0076.png)): Rasterization only: the PDF baselines of Rectangle 8 match within 0.03-0.05 pt (e.g. 348.77 vs 348.74 pt), and the 1600-px renders match to 0.01 px. The baselines fall near a half pixel at 800 px and the sheet rasterizer snaps them opposite ways.
- S0077-3 (slide [77](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0077.png)): Same box as S0076-3. The 800-px-only whole-pixel jump comes from baseline snapping of positions that agree within about 0.05 pt in the PDFs. No Euro-Office layout difference beyond K5.
- S0332-1 (slide [332](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0332.png)): Not a Euro-Office error. The outline has an explicit w=9525, and Euro-Office's PDF strokes the exact geometry (rect bottom at PDF y 140.24523 pt, width 0.74999). PowerPoint's own PDF export rounds the rect origin to 0.01 pt ('132.75 140.25 459 29.081 re'). That 0.0048 pt (0.01 device px) shift puts the stroke edge exactly on a device-pixel centre, so the shared Windows.Data.Pdf rasterizer lights row 887 for PowerPoint and not for Euro-Office. This is rounding in the reference PDF at a pixel-centre tie, not a rendering rule Euro-Office could fix.
- S0402-1 (slide [402](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0402.png)): Not a Euro-Office error. Euro-Office writes the 2.25 pt frame (explicit w=28575) exactly: top at PDF y 387.00003 = EMU 1943100. PowerPoint's PDF writes '546.34 295.38 79.158 91.618 re', so its top lands at 386.998 because the origin is rounded to 0.01 pt. The ideal stroke edge falls exactly on a device-pixel centre (hires 337.5), so a 0.002 pt rounding in the reference decides which row lights. Both PDFs go through the same rasterizer, so this is a tie broken by the reference's coordinate rounding, not an Euro-Office rendering rule.
- S0718-1 (slide [718](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0718.png)): Rasterization only: PDF baselines match within 0.02 pt (line 5: 203.86 vs 203.84 pt; at 800 px = 226.51 vs 226.49 px, straddling the half pixel), and the 1600-px renders match to 0.02 px.
- S0719-1 (slide [719](../../../fidelity-work/decks/great-roll/review/sheets/p0719.png)): Same as S0718-1: PDF baselines 203.86 vs 203.84 pt, 220.06 vs 220.04 pt. The 1-px jump at 800 px comes from the rasterizer rounding a 0.02 pt difference across a half pixel.
